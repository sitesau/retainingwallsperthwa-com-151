Retaining Walls Perth Pros and Cons Explained

Retaining walls in Perth serve different purposes depending on the property's location and soil conditions. On the coastal sand plain, walls often support level building pads or prevent sandy soil erosion. Limestone, a locally quarried material, is frequently used in these areas. In contrast, properties in the Darling Scarp foothills face challenges like steeper slopes, shallow soil over rock, and significant drainage requirements, especially during Perth's wet winters. Understanding these site-specific conditions is crucial for selecting the appropriate wall system, whether it be limestone, post-and-panel concrete, besser block, timber sleeper, or rock and boulder construction.

When comparing retaining wall options, it's beneficial to move beyond simple price-per-metre comparisons. A comprehensive approach involves assessing factors such as approximate retained height, the total length of the wall, site slope, accessibility for machinery, proximity to boundaries, and any related works like paving or fencing that might integrate with the retaining wall. These details help define the project's scope and ensure accurate quoting.

Understanding Project Costs and Scope

The published cost range for installed retaining wall faces in Perth, approximately $250 to $600 per square metre, provides a general guideline. However, it is essential to scrutinise what this figure excludes. Typically, this price does not include GST, excavation, drainage systems, or allowances for difficult site access. Consequently, two quotes that appear similar on the surface can represent vastly different total project costs.

It is vital to clarify whether a quote includes essential elements such as back-of-wall drainage, spoil removal, and site preparation. A lower initial price might not account for these significant components, which could be itemised separately or left for later assessment. Always inquire about the assumed site access for materials and machinery, and confirm whether the quoted price is fixed or subject to site-specific findings. A detailed comparison of complete wall systems, rather than just material costs, will offer a more accurate understanding of the overall investment.

Navigating Approval Requirements

In Western Australia, a building permit is generally required for retaining walls that retain more than 0.5 metres of ground. However, the height of the wall is not the sole determinant for permit requirements. Even walls below this height threshold may necessitate a permit if they are connected to other building works, impact or protect adjoining land, or are situated on a boundary wall or a substantial dividing fence.

The relevant design standard for retaining walls in WA is AS 4678. Projects requiring a permit may also involve a Certificate of Design Compliance (BA3). These requirements indicate that the project may extend beyond a simple landscaping feature and require formal design documentation and a defined approval process. Before finalising material choices, homeowners should document the retained height, note any effects on adjacent properties, and list any associated nearby construction. Confirming these details with the local government authority can streamline the approval process and prevent potential complications later in the project.

Choosing the Right Materials for Perth Conditions

Limestone is a popular choice for retaining walls in Perth, particularly on the Swan Coastal Plain, due to its local availability and suitability for sandy soil conditions. For taller or longer wall installations, concrete post-and-panel systems offer a robust solution. Timber sleeper walls are often used for lower garden terracing projects.

The optimal material selection depends on several factors, including the wall's intended height, the project budget, and the specific ground conditions of the property. Properties in the Darling Scarp foothills, for instance, might require different structural considerations due to slopes and proximity to rock. When comparing proposals, it is advisable to evaluate the entire wall system, including drainage and compliance measures, rather than focusing solely on individual materials. A comprehensive proposal that accounts for site preparation, drainage, and regulatory steps from the outset will provide greater clarity and potentially fewer variations down the line.

  1. Assess Retained Height. Determine the maximum height of the ground the wall will need to retain.
  2. Evaluate Soil and Drainage. Consider the soil type (sandy, clay) and the site's drainage needs, especially in sloped areas.
  3. Check Boundary and Adjacent Land. Note if the wall is near a boundary or will affect neighbouring properties.
  4. Identify Related Works. List any other construction (paving, driveways, fences) that will integrate with the wall.
  5. Consult Local Council. Confirm permit requirements based on height, site conditions, and related works.

Common questions

How much does a retaining wall cost in Perth? Most Perth retaining walls cost between $250 and $600 per square metre of wall face installed. This price varies based on material, height, and site access, and typically excludes GST, excavation, and drainage.

Do I need council approval for a retaining wall in WA? Generally, a building permit is required in Western Australia if a retaining wall retains more than 0.5 metres of ground. Permits may also be needed for walls of any height if they are linked to other building work, affect adjoining land, or are on a boundary.

What is the best retaining wall material for Perth's sandy soil? Limestone is a common and suitable material for Perth's sandy coastal plains. However, the best choice also depends on wall height, budget, and specific site conditions, with concrete post-and-panel and timber sleeper options also being viable.
